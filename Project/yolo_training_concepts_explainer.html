Epoch key term

One full pass through ALL training images

Your dataset had 1024 images. Epoch 1 = model saw all 1024 once. Epoch 21 = model has now seen every image 21 times.

Think of it like a student re-reading a textbook. First read = rough understanding. More reads = better recall. But too many reads of the same book = just memorizing, not learning (overfitting).

Your result: stopped at epoch 21 — early stopping triggered because no improvement after epoch 1.

GPU_mem hardware

GPU RAM used during training

Your T4 has 14.9 GB. You used ~7 GB — comfortable, safe zone.

If this hits 14+ GB → out-of-memory crash. You'd need to reduce batch size (from 16 to 8) to fix it.

Your result: 7 GB / 14.9 GB = 47% usage. Batch size 16 fits well on T4.

box_loss position error

How wrong the bounding box position is

This measures how far off the predicted rectangle is from the actual hand position in the image.

Analogy: You draw a rectangle around someone's hand in a photo. box_loss measures how much your rectangle missed — too far left, too small, etc.

Your epoch 1: 0.354 → epoch 21: 0.381 (slight rise — expected when fine-tuning a good model)

Target: below 0.5 for a well-fitted model. Your values are excellent.

cls_loss identity error

How wrong the class prediction is

This measures how confused the model is about WHICH letter the hand is showing — was it M or N? A or E?

Analogy: The model found the hand correctly, but it said "that's N" when it should be "M". cls_loss counts these wrong guesses.

Your epoch 1: 0.342 → falling over time. Low cls_loss = model confidently knows which letter it sees.

Your result: All classes hitting 99%+ mAP50 means cls_loss is very well controlled.

dfl_loss edge precision

How precisely the box edges align

DFL = Distribution Focal Loss. It's a refinement on top of box_loss — instead of just asking "where is the center of the box", it asks "how precisely do the four edges fall on the exact boundary of the hand?"

Analogy: box_loss = roughly correct rectangle. dfl_loss = are the edges pixel-accurate? It's the fine-tuning of box_loss.

Your values: ~0.91-0.96 throughout — very consistent and low.

Instances batch content

Number of labeled objects in that batch

Each batch of images contains a different number of hand bounding boxes. Instances is just the count of hands detected/labeled across all images in that batch.

Your range: 24-40 per batch. This varies naturally — some images have hands partially visible, some very clearly, affecting how many get counted.

This does not affect accuracy directly — it's just telling you the batch content varied normally.

Size input resolution

Image dimensions fed to the model

All images are resized to 640×640 pixels before being fed to YOLOv8. This is the standard YOLO input size.

Why 640? It's a balance — large enough to see hand details clearly, small enough to process fast. You could use 320 (faster, less accurate) or 1280 (slower, more accurate). 640 is the sweet spot for real-time detection.

Your webcam captures at ~480-720px, so 640 is a perfect match.

mAP50 main accuracy metric

Mean Average Precision at 50% IoU threshold

This is the headline accuracy number for object detection. It combines both "did you find the hand?" and "did you label it correctly?"

IoU (Intersection over Union): how much your predicted box overlaps with the ground-truth box. 50% threshold = if boxes overlap by at least 50%, it counts as a correct detection.

Your result: 99.4% overall — exceptional. For reference, 50% is mediocre, 80% is good, 95%+ is excellent.

mAP50-95 (also shown) is stricter — requires 50% to 95% IoU overlap to count. Your 96.8% on that is also outstanding.

Why did it stop at epoch 21?
Early stopping (patience=20) was set. The best mAP50 was 0.994 at epoch 1. No epoch after that beat this score. After 20 more epochs with no improvement, training stopped automatically. This is normal and healthy — your pretrained model (sign_lang_best.pt) was already so good that fine-tuning converged in the very first pass over the new M and N data.
The three losses working together:
Think of training as a teacher grading three different tests simultaneously.

box_loss = "Did you draw the rectangle in the right place?"
cls_loss = "Did you name the letter correctly?"
dfl_loss = "Are your rectangle edges precisely on the hand boundary?"

Total loss = box_loss + cls_loss + dfl_loss weighted together. Minimizing total loss = getting all three right.

Three loss curves over 21 epochs, all showing a general decrease.
Why does loss sometimes go UP between epochs?
Each epoch shuffles the training data randomly. Some batches are "harder" (similar-looking letters together), which temporarily spikes the loss. This is normal — what matters is the overall downward trend, not perfect smoothness. Your losses all trend downward across 21 epochs. ✓

import pandas as pd import matplotlib.pyplot as plt import matplotlib.gridspec as gridspec # Load YOLOv8 results CSV (auto-saved during training) df = pd.read_csv('/content/runs/AY_v2/results.csv') df.columns = df.columns.str.strip() fig = plt.figure(figsize=(15, 10)) fig.suptitle('YOLOv8 Training Results', fontsize=14, fontweight='bold') gs = gridspec.GridSpec(2, 3, figure=fig, hspace=0.4, wspace=0.35) # mAP50 ax1 = fig.add_subplot(gs[0, 0]) ax1.plot(df['epoch'], df['metrics/mAP50(B)'], color='#2563eb', linewidth=2) ax1.set_title('mAP50'); ax1.set_xlabel('Epoch'); ax1.grid(alpha=0.3) # mAP50-95 ax2 = fig.add_subplot(gs[0, 1]) ax2.plot(df['epoch'], df['metrics/mAP50-95(B)'], color='#16a34a', linewidth=2) ax2.set_title('mAP50-95'); ax2.set_xlabel('Epoch'); ax2.grid(alpha=0.3) # box_loss ax3 = fig.add_subplot(gs[0, 2]) ax3.plot(df['epoch'], df['train/box_loss'], color='#dc2626', linewidth=2, label='train') ax3.plot(df['epoch'], df['val/box_loss'], color='#f97316', linewidth=2, linestyle='--', label='val') ax3.set_title('Box loss');
